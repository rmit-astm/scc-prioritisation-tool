How the prioritisation model works

This page summarises the main assumptions behind the map and benefit estimates. We would particularly like to know where the mapped network, likely treatment, delivery cost or practical feasibility does not match local knowledge.

What would be most useful to check
  • Is a selected corridor genuinely missing or inadequate, or has it already been built or funded?
  • Would protected infrastructure be feasible at the location shown?
  • Is the assumed treatment cost realistic once intersections, parking, drainage, bridges or utilities are considered?
  • Do the modelled order and scale of investment make practical sense?

Please email corrections, evidence or general comments to afshin.jafari@rmit.edu.au. Project IDs shown on the map are helpful when commenting on a location.

Network and candidate projects

  • Strategic corridor network.The current results use the Victorian Government's 2020 Strategic Cycling Corridor geometry. We are obtaining the latest SCC layer and will update the analysis when it is available.
  • Existing infrastructure.Facility status is currently derived from cycling attributes in the MATSim network and spatially reconciled along each SCC. A July 2026 OSM-based refresh is being prepared, so the present map should be treated as a modelled baseline rather than a definitive as-built inventory.
  • Protected is the delivery standard.Separated lanes and off-road paths count as already delivered. Painted lanes and other inadequate facilities are candidates for conversion to protection; locations with no mapped facility are candidates for new protected infrastructure.
  • Traffic stress.Level of traffic stress is derived from facility type, road class, speed and modelled traffic. Investment removes the treated corridor's high-stress exposure.

Delivery costs

  • Permanent P50 case.New protected infrastructure costs $1.00 million/km and conversion of an inadequate facility costs $0.45 million/km.
  • Low-complexity case.New infrastructure costs $0.50 million/km and a straightforward protected retrofit costs $0.05 million/km. This is an optimistic bound, not an assumption that every corridor can be delivered cheaply.
  • Lifecycle costs.The appraisal includes annual maintenance, renewal, residual value and a 10% programme-management allowance over 30 years.
  • Site complexity is not mapped.The kilometre rates do not identify major intersection works, bridges, drainage, lighting, utility relocation, parking removal or other location-specific constraints.

Travel behaviour and bicycle routes

  • Eligible trips.Only existing simulated car trips of 12 km or less can switch to bicycle. Public transport and walking trips are not reassigned.
  • Response to lower traffic stress.The probability of switching is based on a Melbourne mode-choice estimate linking bicycle uptake to the share of a potential route exposed to high traffic stress. Parameter uncertainty is carried into the optimisation.
  • Bicycle route choice.Potential routes use empirically estimated preferences for distance, protected and painted facilities, low-speed streets and slope. The route-choice coefficients come from Greater Bendigo and are tested through alternative specifications.
  • Fixed activities.Origins, destinations and activity locations remain those in the AToM baseline. The model does not represent new destinations or long-run land-use change.

Benefits and appraisal

  • Appraisal frame.Benefits and costs are assessed over 30 years from 2026 using a 7% real discount rate, with 4% and 10% tested as sensitivities. Dashboard values are presented in 2025 Australian dollars.
  • Benefits counted.The model includes avoided climate and air-pollution damage, vehicle operating costs, decongestion, health-system benefits, private health and a treated-share safety benefit.
  • Simulation expansion.The calibrated 10% AToM population sample is expanded by ten and one modelled weekday is annualised using 230 days.
  • Physical emissions.Avoided CO2, NOx, NO2 and PM2.5 are calculated from the simulated car trips estimated to switch. Emissions are reported separately from the broader economic benefits.

How projects are selected

  • Risk-adjusted central case.The central decision rule places 25% weight on outcomes from the weakest 10% of uptake scenarios. This is a decision preference, not a behavioural estimate.
  • Use available funding.This view selects the strongest modelled portfolio within each funding ceiling and will generally continue adding projects as the ceiling increases.
  • Find preferred scale.This view maximises risk-adjusted net benefit and may leave funding unspent when the next available project costs more than its estimated additional benefit.
  • Funding points are independent.Each level is solved separately. Moving the slider does not represent an agreed construction sequence, and a project selected at one level is not guaranteed to remain selected at the next.

Important effects not quantified

  • Wider network effects.The model does not value benefits to existing bicycle riders, generated bicycle trips, safety-in-numbers, equity, amenity or network-learning effects.
  • Land use and destinations.Changes in access to schools, stations, activity centres and other destinations are not modelled, nor is the additional uptake that might result from coordinated transport and land-use planning.
  • Engineering feasibility.The selected lines are strategic investment options, not designs, funded projects or construction recommendations. Local review is needed before interpreting any location as deliverable.
