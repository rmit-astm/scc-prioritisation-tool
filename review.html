Investment explorer Model assumptions

Assumptions currently used

This is a working summary of the inputs used in the dashboard. The network and results will change after the 2026 infrastructure and SCC update.

Data dates

  • SCC network:the current candidate network uses the Victorian Government's 2020 Strategic Cycling Corridor layer.
  • Existing bicycle facilities:the current results use cycling attributes held in the MATSim network. These attributes came from an older OSM extract whose exact date was not recorded.
  • 2026 network update:a Greater Melbourne OSM extract downloaded on 23 July 2026 is available and will be used when the latest SCC layer is received.
  • Travel demand and emissions:car trips, routes and emissions come from the calibrated 10% AToM baseline. This is not a newly calibrated 2026 travel-demand model.
  • Economic appraisal:the appraisal starts in 2026. Dashboard monetary values are expressed in 2025 Australian dollars.

Dashboard scenarios

  • Baseline:zero new funding. The map shows the bicycle facilities represented in the current model network.
  • Permanent P50:uses the central Victorian benchmark construction and lifecycle costs.
  • Low complexity:uses lower costs for straightforward protected construction or retrofit. It is an optimistic cost case and still assumes a protected treatment.
  • Use available funding:selects the portfolio with the largest modelled benefit within the selected funding ceiling.
  • Find preferred scale:selects the portfolio with the largest risk-adjusted net benefit. It can leave part of the funding ceiling unspent.
  • Funding levels:each point on the slider is optimised separately. The points are not a staged construction sequence.

Projects and treatments

  • Existing protected:separated on-road lanes, bicycle paths and shared paths are treated as already delivered.
  • Upgrade candidate:a painted lane, shared-street treatment or other inadequate facility is converted to protected infrastructure when selected.
  • New construction:a corridor with no mapped facility is treated as new protected infrastructure when selected.
  • Traffic stress:link stress is based on facility type, road class, speed and modelled traffic. Selected projects are assumed to remove high-stress exposure on the treated section.
  • Project conflicts:parallel project representations covering the same corridor section cannot both be selected.

Cost assumptions

  • Permanent P50 — new:$1.00 million per km.
  • Permanent P50 — upgrade:$0.45 million per km.
  • Low complexity — new:$0.50 million per km.
  • Low complexity — upgrade:$0.05 million per km.
  • Maintenance:$7,500/km/year for new infrastructure and $7,000/km/year for upgrades.
  • Asset life:25 years for new construction and 15 years for upgrades.
  • Other lifecycle assumptions:100% renewal at the end of asset life, straight-line residual value and a 10% programme-management allowance.
  • Not separately costed:major intersections, bridges, drainage, lighting, utilities, parking changes and other site-specific works.

Travel behaviour and routing

  • Maximum trip distance:only existing simulated car trips of 12 km or less can switch to bicycle.
  • Mode response:the reduction in high-stress route exposure is converted into a car-to-bicycle switching probability using a Melbourne mode-choice coefficient.
  • Coefficient uncertainty:the central high-stress coefficient is −0.768 with a standard deviation of 0.164.
  • General uptake uncertainty:a programme-wide lognormal multiplier with sigma 0.25 is applied across behavioural scenarios.
  • Bicycle routes:route costs use Greater Bendigo estimates for distance, protected facilities, painted lanes, low-speed streets and slope.
  • Fixed travel pattern:trip origins, destinations and activity locations remain those in the AToM baseline.

Benefits and appraisal

  • Appraisal period:30 years from 2026.
  • Discount rate:7% real in the central case, with 4% and 10% used as sensitivities.
  • Benefits included:climate, air pollution, vehicle operating costs, decongestion, health-system benefits, private health and bicycle safety.
  • Safety:uses a 40% risk reduction, the treated share of the bicycle route and the rule of half for new users.
  • Simulation scaling:the 10% AToM sample is multiplied by 10 and the modelled weekday is annualised using 230 days.
  • Emissions:avoided CO2, NOx, NO2 and PM2.5 come from the simulated car trips estimated to switch.

Optimisation

  • Central risk setting:25% weight is placed on the mean result from the weakest 10% of uptake scenarios.
  • Risk parameter:the 25% weight is a decision setting, not an estimated behavioural parameter.
  • Benefit evaluation:reported portfolios are evaluated with behavioural draws that were not used to select them.
  • Solution status:the portfolios are best-found solutions. Valid global bounds are calculated, but the full nonlinear solutions are not proven global optima.

Not included in the quantified results

  • Benefits to people already riding bicycles.
  • New trips generated by the completed bicycle network.
  • Switching from public transport or walking.
  • Safety-in-numbers, equity and amenity effects.
  • Changes in land use, destinations or long-term activity patterns.
  • Detailed engineering feasibility and site-specific designs.
